Information Systems & Information Management

An Information System (IS) is an organized combination of people, hardware, software, data, procedures, and networks that collects, processes, stores, and distributes information to support decision-making and business operations.

Overview

Information Management is the process of collecting, organizing, storing, protecting, retrieving, and using information efficiently. Its core purpose is to improve productivity, support decision-making, protect valuable information, and ensure easy access to information.

The Six Components of an Information System

An Information System consists of six vital elements:

01

Hardware

Physical devices used in an information system.

02

Software

Programs and applications that tell hardware what to do.

03

Data

Raw facts collected and processed into useful information.

04

People

Users who interact with the information system.

05

Procedures

Rules and steps guiding users in operating the system.

06

Networks

Connects computers and devices to share information.

/ 04

Five Major Types of Information Systems

  • Transaction Processing System (TPS): Records, processes, and stores routine day-to-day business transactions (e.g., ATM transactions, Payroll, POS system).
  • Management Information System (MIS): Collects data from TPS and converts it into meaningful reports (e.g., Monthly sales & inventory reports).
  • Decision Support System (DSS): Designed to help managers analyze data, evaluate alternatives, and make decisions for complex or non-routine problems (e.g., Sales forecasting, Budget planning).
  • Executive Information System (EIS): Provides top executives with easy access to summarized and critical performance information through dashboards and charts.
  • Office Automation System (OAS): Uses software and communication tech to automate routine office tasks (e.g., Microsoft Word, Google Workspace, Zoom).
/ 05

The Information Lifecycle

The stages information goes through from creation until disposal:

  • 1. Creation or Collection: Information is created or collected from various sources like forms or transactions.
  • 2. Storage: Securely stored in databases, files, or cloud storage.
  • 3. Use/Processing: Accessed and analyzed to support daily operations and decisions.
  • 4. Sharing/Distribution: Distributed to authorized users or departments to improve collaboration.
  • 5. Archiving / Retention: Retained securely for legal, historical, or future reference.
  • 6. Disposal / Destruction: Securely deleted or destroyed when retention periods end to protect privacy.
/ 06

Information Quality

Information Quality refers to how useful, reliable, and accurate information is for decision-making. High-quality information features:

  • Accurate: Correct, error-free, reflecting reality.
  • Complete: Contains all necessary details.
  • Relevant: Directly useful for the purpose.
  • Timely: Up-to-date and available when needed.
  • Reliable: From trustworthy and dependable sources.
  • Consistent: Uniform and non-contradictory across reports.
